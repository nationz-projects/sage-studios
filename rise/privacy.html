Rise privacy policy

Last updated 29 October 2026

Rise is an alarm clock. It has no accounts and no ads, and the developer never receives your personal data. Everything you set up stays on your phone.

What stays on your phone

  • Your alarms and settings are saved only on your device.
  • Calendar (optional, Morning screen): if you turn it on, Rise reads today's events on your phone to show them after your alarm. They are never sent anywhere.
  • Physical activity (optional, Steps mission): Rise counts steps with your phone's own sensor while the alarm is ringing. Nothing is stored or sent.
  • Camera (optional, Scan mission): Rise reads a barcode or QR code on your phone to turn the alarm off. No photo or video is saved or sent.

What leaves your phone

  • Weather (optional, Morning screen): if you turn it on, Rise sends your approximate location, rounded to about 1 km, to Open-Meteo to get the day's forecast. This happens when an alarm rings. Rise sends no name, account or device identifier. Like any website, Open-Meteo can see the request's IP address; see their privacy policy for how they handle it.
  • Purchases: Rise Plus is bought through Google Play. Google handles payment; Rise only learns whether Plus is active on your Google account.
  • Scanner diagnostics: the Scan mission uses Google's ML Kit barcode scanner, which sends Google basic diagnostics (phone model, Android version, app version, a random per-install identifier, and how long a scan took) to keep the scanner working. It never includes the camera image, the code or anything about you. See Google's ML Kit disclosure.

Permissions

Rise asks for alarms, notifications, full-screen alerts and display over other apps so alarms ring on time and take over the screen. Location, calendar, physical activity and camera are only requested when you switch on the feature that uses them, and you can turn them off at any time in Rise or in your phone's settings.

Children

Rise is not directed at children under 13.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Rise removes everything it stored. Since the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing else to delete.
